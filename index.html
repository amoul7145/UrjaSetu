Understand an oil well before changing the real one.

Explore a simple computer model of a thick-oil well. Change imaginary heating and pump settings, then see how the simulated results change.

No real well is connected. This is an educational prototype, not an engineering prediction or operating recommendation.

The problem in three pictures

🌡️ 1. Thick oil Some underground oil is difficult to move when it is cool.
→
♨️ 2. Heating Steam can warm the underground area and help the oil flow.
→
⚙️ 3. Pumping A pump brings oil to the surface. Heating and pumping affect one another.

Engineers need to understand these connected changes. A digital twin is a computer model that represents them using measurements and equations. This demo shows the idea with deliberately simplified example calculations.

INPUT

Well data

🔍 What happens behind the screen?

1. Enter data You move the three sliders. In a future real project, sensors and historical records would provide the data.
→
2. Run a model Simple demonstration formulas update the warmth, flow and energy indexes.
→
3. See the result The charts and numbers update instantly so you can compare two imaginary scenarios.

What a real digital twin would still need

Real data: measurements and operating history from an oil well.

Engineering model: verified calculations for underground heating, fluid movement and pump behaviour.

Validation: checking predicted values against real measurements and expert review.

Future AI: only after suitable training data and meaningful testing are available.

Secure deployment: access controls, reliable data connections and safety review.
